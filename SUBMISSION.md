**Logline:** A shortwave radio for islands that aren't there. Turn the dial; the stations bleed into each other.

**Description:**
Forecast for Nowhere is a listening game played on one control: a shortwave dial. Six stations read the weather for six
islands that appear on no chart. One island has apples that fall upward, one is a volcano, one is a glassmakers' island, and one
broadcasts from under the sea. The islands mention each other, and they all mention a lighthouse on a rock with no name.
The stations sit close together on the dial, so tuning is physical mixing. Between two stations you hear both voices at
once, and the subtitles and paintings tear and overlap in the same way. When you are slightly off-frequency, a heterodyne
whistle rises and falls. Stay on a broadcast long enough and it goes in your log. Log all six and a seventh station appears
in the gap between two of the others, where you could only hear faint counting pips before. The whole interface is the
radio. There are no menus or buttons during play, only listening.

**Why it fits "Experimental & Open Format"** (paste after the description if there is room):
- Break the mold: the whole interface is one radio dial. There are no menus, buttons or choices during play, only tuning and listening.
- Clear intent: a lonely, strange shipping forecast for places that don't exist. Six voices, one shared mystery (the lighthouse), and a seventh station you earn by listening.
- Craft: the sound mix IS the game state. Signal strength drives voice gain, filter cutoff, distortion, static, whistle pitch,
  picture tearing and subtitle dropout all at once. Progress comes from attention (time actually spent listening), not from reflexes.
- The gameplay video opens on the dial sweeping through static into the first station (the core mechanic in the first 8 s).
